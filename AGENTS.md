- **A share split is not a ninety per cent loss, and the app applies it the morning it happens.** Nvidia split ten for one on 10 June 2024: a hundred shares became a thousand at a tenth of the price and nothing was traded. Every price this app can read is post-split from that open, so a `portfell_holdings` row still saying a hundred is valued at a tenth of the truth, and it does not stay on one screen: that figure reaches **Pulse**, which reads it as a thesis breaking, the alerts, and the **Sunday letter**, which states its numbers as fact and would tell somebody in plain English that their week was a catastrophe. A reverse split does the same in the other direction and reads as a windfall. Migration `20260824120000_share_splits.sql` is the answer: `portfell_share_splits` is the ledger and its primary key makes applying one twice impossible, `portfell_split_checks` is the day's claim so one worker asks the provider, and `portfell_apply_split` does the arithmetic in one transaction. **The share count goes up and every per-share figure on the row goes down**: the buy price, the end of year target and any hand-set stock target, because all three are prices of one share and a $1,000 target on a stock that now trades at $100 is not a target anybody set. Fractions are kept rather than paid out, because Upside Lab holds fractional shares already. `fetchSplits` (`src/lib/market/yahoo.ts`) returns **null when it could not ask and an empty list when it asked and nothing happened**, and `applyDueSplits` hands the day's claim back on the first, because otherwise a provider having a bad minute costs a whole day of everybody's holdings being wrong. The cron is `0 15 * * 1-5`, which is after the opening bell in both halves of the year. Upside Arena has the same feature for the same reason (its migration `0027`), with one difference that is not a preference: Arena holds whole shares only, so it pays the leftover fraction out in cash at the post-split price, and this one does not have to.

What moved picks the five biggest moves by size and then reads them best-to-worst (sorting by percent alone dropped the name that caused the week); suggestions are grouped one section per kind by `groupSuggestions`, and the letterhead is the lockup and date on one line over a hairline, with the week's figure and Margus's paragraphs sharing one opening card. **Separators are the English comma everywhere, and the preview line leads with the percent.** Gmail rebuilds the inbox snippet with its own text pass that strips the separators inside a number, so mail reading `-$27,357 this week` showed up as `-$27357` under a subject that rendered the comma perfectly. (The bare `—` glyph that `format.ts` returns for a missing value is a different thing and stays.) One spacing scale runs the whole letter: `ROW_PAD` for table rows and `CARD_ROW_PAD` inside cards, applied to **both** sides of every hairline — a divider padded on one side only is what made two suggestions in one card look mis-set. What moved carries a **move bar** in the middle column (`moveBar`): a grey track the full width of the channel, a zero line at its centre, and the week's move growing out of it, gains right and losses left, scaled to the biggest move in that table. Tables and `bgcolor` only, never a `div` with a width, and the fill paints over the track cell's own background because email has no z-order. **The fill is a percentage of its half of the channel, never a pixel width**, and it tops out at 88% so the biggest move still stops short of the end: a fixed 116px bar is two thirds of the 173px half a 560px letter gives it and the whole of the 81px half a phone gives it, which is why the biggest mover ran edge to edge on a phone and looked clipped.
- **The unsubscribe link in the Sunday letter stops the Sunday letter, rather than pointing at a sign-in page.** It was `https://upsidelab.app/account` for everybody, which asks the reader it is written for, somebody who has stopped opening Upside Lab, to sign back into the account they left and find a switch. - **A letter states its numbers as fact, so a letter whose numbers are thin is not sent.** `weeklyNumbersAreSound` (`src/lib/weekly-letter.ts`) runs per recipient before anything is claimed or written, and `dispatchWeeklyLetters` passes that person over (logging `sunday_letter_skipped_untrusted` with the reason) rather than mailing a confident wrong figure; they keep their empty marker, so a later Sunday slot retries with fresh data. - **Borrowed money is a size question, and the answer decides how loud the card gets.** Negative cash used to be one flat alarm: `alerts.ts` fired at anything under -$500 and `CashAlertCard` drew it in `border-loss/30 bg-loss/10` with a warning triangle, saying "Cash is -$20,000." and nothing more. That is the same rose panel for somebody borrowing 8% of their portfolio, which is an ordinary way to hold one, as for somebody borrowing 60%, who is about two bad weeks from a forced sale, and it never told either of them the number that separates the two. A card that is red whatever the arithmetic says is a card the reader learns to swipe past, which is the last thing you want the day it starts being right. `src/lib/margin-health.ts` is the answer and it is pure. The size that matters is the loan against **what the portfolio is actually worth** (stocks plus cash, so the loan is already netted out), not against nothing: under **30%** is a normal amount to carry and stays neutral, 30% to **50%** takes a warning ring, and 50% and up is the only tier that goes rose and the only one that says "margin call" out loud. A loan larger than the portfolio has no share to quote (any percentage of the reader's own money is either enormous or the wrong sign) and goes straight to the top tier on that fact alone. **The distance to the floor is the whole point of the change**: `dropToMarginCall` is closed form, `f = 1 - borrowed / (stocks * (1 - m))`, which at m = 0.5 is just `1 - 2 * borrowed / stocks`. **`MAINTENANCE_RATE` is 0.5 on purpose and it is not the real number** (brokers use 25% to 30%, and raise it without notice on exactly the concentrated, volatile name most likely to be reading this) so the copy says which floor it assumed: a cushion that turns out bigger than promised costs nobody anything, and one that turns out smaller is a forced sale. `book-shock.ts` models a scenario against the standard 30% and stays as it is; this is the resting number on the Cash card. Every tier ends on a move the reader can make, because an alert that says a number is bad and stops has handed somebody a worry and no answer. Two things not to undo: the alert id carries its tier (`decision-margin-heavy`), because dismissals are stored per id and one id for every size of loan meant somebody who waved off a calm note at 10% never heard again at 60%; and the card is `card-sheen glass` with a **ring**, never a tinted fill, per the card-wash rule above, with the alarm triangle reserved for the two tiers that earned it. `margin-health.test.ts` checks the arithmetic against a simulated fall rather than against itself.
- **Cash goes below zero on every portfolio, and the sign is a toggle rather than a minus you type.** A brokerage account that lent somebody the money to buy with carries the loan as negative cash, which is why the canonical Aasad portfolio above is cash -7000 and why `alerts.ts` has said "part of your portfolio is borrowed" under -$500 all along. Four separate places used to refuse it on anything but a classroom sheet, and together they were worse than any one of them: `sheetCashBalance` clamped the reading at zero, so a reader who owed $7,000 was told they owed nothing **and** had that $7,000 added back to their portfolio value, in Pulse, the letter and every total; `GET /api/portfolios` went further and *wrote* the zero back over the stored number, so the truth did not survive a page load; the PATCH refused the save; and the modal refused it before that. All four are gone. Only the size is still capped, by `isSafeSignedMoney` on both sides. What stays is `tracksTradeCash`, which is a different question entirely: whether a buy or a sell moves cash at all, and only a classroom sheet does that. **The phone half is not a detail.** `inputMode="decimal"` opens the number pad, and on iOS that pad is digits and a dot with no minus key anywhere on it, so even on a paper sheet where a negative was allowed there was no way to enter one. `CashModal` asks for the amount and takes the sign from a two-way toggle, "Money there" / "Money borrowed" (a pasted `-7000` flips it rather than landing in the field), which is also plainer than a minus for somebody who has never thought of borrowed money as a negative number. - **More than one session works this repo at a time, so read what landed before you merge.** On 2026-08-24 two sessions built share-split handling within an hour of each other. Both were sound, both passed CI, both merged, and together they were a data-corruption bug: `portfell_apply_split` is keyed on `(ticker, effective_on)` **globally** and moves every holding of that company, while the other implementation offered the reader a button that wrote the same correction through `/api/holdings` without touching the ledger. A reader who pressed it before the cron ran would have had the split applied twice, so a hundred shares became ten thousand rather than a thousand. It was caught only because a merge returned `405 Base branch was modified` and that prompted a look at *why*, which is luck rather than method.
  So: **when `main` has moved under your branch, read the commits before merging, not just the conflict status.** A clean three-way merge is exactly what you get when two people build the same feature in different files, and it is the case that hurts. A pane whose blacks are grey is a card, not glass. **`--glass-contrast` (1.05) is the second lever and is not optional** — contrast pivots on mid-grey so it leaves true black alone, but this field is not true black (the ambient lobes sit at about five levels) and without it a pane lifts those six: measured 1.00 → +6.1, 1.02 → +3.1, 1.05 → +0.1. **Saturation is free**: 1.75 and 2.40 measure identically on black lift, so richness costs nothing. `--glass-blur` is **6px, not 40** — past roughly 7px a blur stops refracting and starts mixing adjacent hues into mud, which is the same mechanism that produced the cast. The rim came down with the fill (`--glass-rim-top` 38%, not 56%) because at this transparency the old weight read as a drawn outline rather than as light; it still takes the room's two hues, warm on top from `--primary`, cool underneath from `--ambient-cool`, with the sides unequal because the key light is off the left. **Wells are panes in their own right** (`--glass-veil-well` 3%, own 5px blur, the softest rim in the system) rather than grey boxes on a pane, because forty holdings rows must not add up to a texture. **Overlays and the dock are the surfaces that keep a heavy fill and a hard blur** — they sit over real content, which is both why they must soften it and where a heavy blur earns its keep (`.glass-overlay` at `--popover` 78%, `.glass-dock` at `--background` 55%; the dock's is field-keyed, so its alpha is exactly how much of the ambient glow it eats and it cannot leave a hue behind). Never reintroduce a falloff, a `background-image` ramp, or a tinted card fill on any glass surface. Judge a change by measuring black lift and the material's own chroma on real surfaces, never by eyeballing.
- **Design system: see `DESIGN_TOKENS.md` for the full account.** True-black field (`--background`), shadcn's own card/popover step (`--card`, `oklch(0.205 0 0)` — do not darken it further), hairline borders (`--border`, white at 16% alpha, not a fill). Primary accent is a **subtle warm yellow** (`--primary`), muted and low-chroma on purpose. The only other colours are semantic and fixed: emerald `--gain`, rose `--loss`/`--destructive`, orange `--warning`. Don't reach for a new Tailwind hue decoratively — the Accent Palette in `DESIGN_TOKENS.md` is a ceiling, not a starting point; add to that file first if a fix seems to need a new colour. Two typefaces split by job — Geist for every sentence, Geist Mono for every figure, **Archivo** for headings and the wordmark; heading tracking is a scale, not a constant. Real shadcn primitives (`Card`, `Table`, `Badge`, `Progress`, `Dialog`, `Select`) over hand-rolled divs; semantic tokens (`bg-card`, `border-border`, `text-muted-foreground`) over literal colours; the 4px/8px spacing scale over arbitrary `p-[13px]` values; **a `Panel` spaces its own children (`gap-5 sm:gap-6`), so a direct child must not add `mt-*`/`mb-*` — it gets both** (a title and its subtitle are one wrapped child, and a component never carries its own outer margin); `font-mono tabular-nums` on every dollar amount, percentage and share count. No tinted card-background washes (`bg-<hue>-950/20` and similar) — status is a border accent or a `Badge`, never a fill. Top-level cards use `.glass` and nested wells `.glass-well` (see `BOX`/`CARD`/`SCORE_CELL` in `Panel.tsx`); don't reintroduce a flat opaque `bg-card`/`bg-muted` on a top-level card, since that is what lets the ambient corner glow read *through* card surfaces rather than only in the gutters. The glow lives on `.page-frame` and follows the tokens automatically; `--ambient-cool` is chrome-only and has no Tailwind utility, so don't reach for it on a component. **Balance the two lobes on Helmholtz–Kohlrausch-corrected lightness (L\*\*), never on brightest channel or luminance** — a saturated blue appears far brighter than a yellow of the same luminance, so a pair that measures balanced can read 19% apart; see `DESIGN_TOKENS.md` for the model and the numbers. **The warm accent cannot be a dark tint** — yellow only reads as yellow while it is light, so a low-alpha `--primary` veil over the near-black field lands on khaki and dim yellow type on it is barely a contrast step. Selected surfaces either fill with `--primary` at full lightness and take `--primary-foreground` type (the dock cell, a `Segmented` item) or stay neutral with `--foreground` type (`WorkspaceSwitcher`, the one selected state that must not compete with the header's CTA). There is no middle. **Nothing that is a surface may be a flat fill** — `bg-muted` as a container background reads as a hole beside a glass card, so use `card-sheen glass-well`. Meter tracks, heatmap and data cells, skeletons, step dots and `focus:bg-muted` stay opaque on purpose. Overlays — menus, selects, popovers, dialogs, sheets, drawers — are `.glass-overlay`, the same material at a much heavier fill (`--popover` at 88% against a card's 45%), because they sit *over* content and an element with `backdrop-filter` does not blur what another one painted, so a menu under the chrome sees the market strip unblurred. Glass carries three specular terms (top hairline, a soft inset falloff under it, bottom hairline); the falloff is the refraction and on a card it is a `box-shadow`, never a `background-image`, because `.veil-hover` would wipe a gradient on hover. `.chrome-pane` is the one exception and uses a gradient on purpose — it is a band of two different heights and a shadow spread is a fixed distance, so it would hug the edge on one and wash half the band on the other; nothing in the chrome hovers.
- **The phone is its own room, not a narrow desktop — full account in `DESIGN_TOKENS.md`.** Below `md` the ambient lobes come in and up, and glass thins, so the corner light reaches through the cards; the phone chrome is **one element**, not one look — `AppHeader`'s `.chrome-pane` holds the phone top bar, the desktop header row and the market strip, because a `backdrop-filter` samples its own slice of the backdrop and two stacked panes land at different tones with a seam between them (`MobileTopBar` is a bare row now; giving it a fill or blur again brings the seam back). The only edge is at the pane's bottom, as on desktop; the page gutter, `PANEL_PAD`, `NESTED_PAD` and `SCORE_CELL` step to 16px below `sm` and figures to `text-xl`. Hard rules: **figures wrap, never `whitespace-nowrap`** (that is how `23.0% a year` escaped its card); **a small card fitting two pairs of values uses two spread rows, never two stacked columns**, sized to fit with `flex-wrap` as the floor (columns share one width, so the `min-w-0` side starves and clips); **a ticker chip heading a column of text gets `.chip-hang`**, opt-in and never on `Badge` itself; put the unit in the sub-line and pass `Scoreboard mobileCols={1}` for any row whose cells carry a sentence. `overscroll-behavior-y: none` on `html, body` is load-bearing — without it WebKit's bounce drags the sticky top bar down and slices the tab bar in half. **When checking for clipping, measure against the nearest clipping ancestor's padding box**, not just `scrollWidth` and the viewport; `.chip-hang` is the one sanctioned exception. Touch targets are pointer-gated in `globals.css` and cover buttons, inputs, selects and `[data-slot="select-trigger"]` — but **never `.inline-edit`** (table rows are a fixed `h-10`) and never the checkbox/switch pseudo (it would steal taps from the rows either side). A size class on an `<h1>`–`<h4>` works now that the element rules are in `@layer base`; `src/lib/heading-scale.test.ts` fails if one disagrees with the scale.
- **A forecast on a phone is a shape first, and it never drops a year.** The holding cards were a two-column grid of five editable prices with today's price sitting in it as a sixth peer, and they filtered the last forecast year out so the columns divided evenly (`mobileYears = yearCols.filter((y) => y !== 2030)`) while the percentage in each card's own corner was measured **to that dropped year** — so `$CRWV 382.1%` sat above a card whose last figure was `+290%`, and a card that cannot add up to its own headline is worse than one that says less. The whole-portfolio block had the same hole: its `To End 2030` line was `hidden md:block` inside a `md:hidden` container, which is to say dead since it was written. Time also ran in a Z, 2026 top right and 2027 bottom left, because two columns give a sequence two directions. Now each card leads with `TickerSpark`, a 56px path from today to the last year with a dashed rule at today's price so above and below read without a number being involved, and one spread row naming both ends; **Show every year** opens `YearRail`, which lists Now and every year down the page with that year's gain beside it. The card went from about 700px to about 230px, so four holdings fit on a phone where one used to, which is the whole point of a five-year forecast: comparing names. **Deliberately not `SheetPathChart`** — that one belongs to the portfolio and carries an axis, a drag readout and 224px of height, and five stacked would be taller than the grid they replaced. The rail's price fields are `EoyPriceInput fill`, **bounded at `w-24`, never `w-full`**: the edit cue is a dashed `border-bottom` on the element itself, so a full-width field draws a rule clean across the row and reads as a separator, which `globals.css` warns about in as many words. Height sits on the row (`min-h-12`), not the input, because an input given a 44px min-height centres its digits and leaves the dash floating ten pixels below them. `src/lib/forecast-years.test.ts` fails on a hardcoded forecast year or on a second, narrower list of years anywhere in the panel: the years are a range that moves, and a layout that only fits four of them is a layout problem, never a reason to show three quarters of a forecast.
