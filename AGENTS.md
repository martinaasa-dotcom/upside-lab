- **A share split is not a ninety per cent loss, and the app applies it the morning it happens.** Nvidia split ten for one on 10 June 2024: a hundred shares became a thousand at a tenth of the price and nothing was traded. Every price this app can read is post-split from that open, so a `portfell_holdings` row still saying a hundred is valued at a tenth of the truth, and it does not stay on one screen: that figure reaches **Pulse**, which reads it as a thesis breaking, the alerts, and the **Sunday letter**, which states its numbers as fact and would tell somebody in plain English that their week was a catastrophe. A reverse split does the same in the other direction and reads as a windfall. Migration `20260824120000_share_splits.sql` is the answer: `portfell_share_splits` is the ledger and its primary key makes applying one twice impossible, `portfell_split_checks` is the day's claim so one worker asks the provider, and `portfell_apply_split` does the arithmetic in one transaction. **The share count goes up and every per-share figure on the row goes down**: the buy price, the end of year target and any hand-set stock target, because all three are prices of one share and a $1,000 target on a stock that now trades at $100 is not a target anybody set. Fractions are kept rather than paid out, because Upside Lab holds fractional shares already. `fetchSplits` (`src/lib/market/yahoo.ts`) returns **null when it could not ask and an empty list when it asked and nothing happened**, and `applyDueSplits` hands the day's claim back on the first, because otherwise a provider having a bad minute costs a whole day of everybody's holdings being wrong. The cron is `0 15 * * 1-5`, which is after the opening bell in both halves of the year. Upside Arena has the same feature for the same reason (its migration `0027`), with one difference that is not a preference: Arena holds whole shares only, so it pays the leftover fraction out in cash at the post-split price, and this one does not have to.

- **A forecast is allowed to point down, and nothing in the app moves the model's number any more (2026-08-28).** Three separate mechanisms used to floor every path at the shape kept for its kind of business, and together they were worse than any one of them. `liftPathToThemeMagnitude` scaled a path up when its last year came in under the theme shape and **replaced a falling path outright**; the near-linear check substituted the theme path wholesale, so a steady decline was detected as a straight line and handed back as a rise; and `restoreCurrentYearDestination` rewrote a flat current year whenever the theme still had a move left in it. **Measured on the real function**: a model answering `92, 78, 84, 70, 61` against a $100 spot, which is a 39% fall, reached the reader as `139, 203, 182, 275, 357`, a 257% gain. Not softened, inverted. `FORECAST_CONVICTION_PROMPT` had said all along that "a path that ends below today" is an allowed answer and told the model in as many words not to round one up "out of politeness", so this was the post-processing contradicting the prompt, silently, on the one output where being wrong costs somebody money. What is left is gap-filling, which only ever writes a year the model did not, and `reshapeToThemeRhythm`, which re-times an even ramp onto the theme's rhythm by log-interpolating to **the model's own final price**, so the shape rule survives without carrying a magnitude opinion inside it. Do not reintroduce a floor, a lift, a minimum multiple, or a "never below the index" rule: `forecast-floor.test.ts` asserts a declining path, a flat path and a modest path all reach the reader untouched, and `provenance.test.ts` fails if any reader copy starts promising a floor again. It was found by writing the transparency panel: the adjustment could not be described honestly, which is the argument for describing them all.

Adding is the service role's work (the check is the whole job, and a client checking itself is not a check); the one write a client may make to that table is deleting a row of its own, and row level security says so. **The one destructive case is narrow and the database decides it.** An address that already has an Upside Lab account can be taken only when `portfell_account_never_used` says that account has no name, no answers to the experience questions, nothing bought, no portfolio owned or co-owned, no circle, no join request, no conviction notes or watchlist and no seed portfolio waiting on it, which is somebody who signed in once and closed the tab. - **A cache one reader fills and every reader drinks from needs a way to go stale.** `portfell_forecast_ticker_cache` shares a five-year price path across portfolios so the same company is not re-reasoned every time it appears, which is right. As it first landed it was keyed on the ticker and nothing else: no age, no tie to the price the path grew out of, and a `generated_at` that was re-stamped every time the row was handed out. The first portfolio ever to hold a name would have fixed that name's forecast for every reader in the product, permanently. Three rules keep it honest and all three are load-bearing (`src/lib/forecast-ticker-cache-store.ts`). A row **ages out at 14 days**, because one shared run serves everybody and re-reasoning a name twice a month costs nothing next to a path that can never be revisited. A row carries the **share price it was reasoned from** and is refused once the stock has moved a **fifth** from it, because a year-one target only means something next to the price it grew out of. And `generated_at` is **when the model reasoned the path, never when it was last reused**, or a popular ticker becomes immortal and the age bound can never fire. The write-back takes **only what the model actually returned this run**: `ensureCompleteEoyTargets` hands back a row for every holding, filling names the model never mentioned from the generic shaper, and publishing those would serve a shape to every other reader as though it had been reasoned and stop the model ever being asked about that name again. - **A short coin alias is also a listed company, and which one the reader meant is settled by whether they picked it.** BTC is a Grayscale trust, SOL is Emeren Group, LINK is Interlink Electronics. `scoreTickerSuggestion` already settles the ambiguity deliberately and correctly: typing the alias ranks the coin first and the company stays one row down. Somebody who owns a solar company would have had it priced as Solana, in their portfolio value, in Pulse, in the forecast and in the Sunday letter. So `tickerFieldText` prettifies a **stored coin symbol and nothing else**, and both places a holding is created (`HoldingModal`, `WelcomeTour`) **remember the symbol the reader picked** rather than re-deriving it from what is on screen, clearing it the moment they type, because then the text is what they meant.
- **The forged-request gate covers every mutation, not just `/api/*`.** It used to sit inside the `isApi` branch in `src/proxy.ts`, which left `/auth/email/complete` (a POST on a page path that mints a session) as the one mutating route in the app with nothing in front of it. `SameSite=Lax` does not help there: the danger is not the victim's cookie being sent, it is a forged post **setting** one, so a stranger's sign-in token could be posted through somebody else's browser and leave that person typing their holdings into the stranger's account. Callers with no browser behind them (Stripe's signed webhook, Gmail's one-click unsubscribe post) still pass, for the reasons `same-origin.ts` gives.
- **A figure this app states as fact is never rounded up into existence.** `aboutMove` and `aboutPct` (`src/lib/book-insights.ts`, `src/lib/morning-read.ts`) read `Math.max(1, Math.round(pct * 100))`, so a group that moved two tenths of a percent was printed on Home as "up about 1%", five times what happened. Below a whole percent they say **"less than 1%"**, which fits every sentence these build and is both shorter and true. The floor on `sharePct` stays: that is a share of the portfolio, its callers filter at 15%, and "0% of your portfolio" would be the worse sentence.
- **Nothing assumes a response carries every row it matched.** PostgREST answers with at most `db-max-rows`, which a Supabase project is set to **1,000** by default, and it applies that **silently**: no error, no flag, a shorter list. Nothing in this repository had ever mentioned the number, and every read that has to be complete was written as one request. **The nightly snapshot is the worst of them**: `captureBookPayload` (`src/lib/book-snapshot.ts`) is called by the cron with **no ids at all**, which is every portfolio and every holding in the project, and a backup missing most of its rows while looking exactly like a good one is worse than no backup. `docs/DISASTER_RECOVERY.md` rests on those. **The Sunday letter is the loudest**: batching turned four queries per recipient into four queries total, which is right, and it also made each one grow with the mailing list. A hundred readers with four portfolios of twenty-five names is **10,000 holding rows**, so most letters would have been written from part of a portfolio, and `weeklyNumbersAreSound` **cannot catch that**: it refuses on a holding with no quote, and a truncated holding is not present to have one. The letter would state a portfolio value computed from a fraction of the portfolio, as a fact, in an inbox. **And an export is a legal answer**, so `src/lib/gdpr/user-export.ts` is paged too, cash events first since nothing prunes them. They use `readAll` (`src/lib/supabase/read-all.ts`), which pages at **500**, deliberately below the cap because a loop asking for exactly its limit cannot tell a full page from a truncated one. **The second argument is the whole design decision**: `"stop"` hands back what it read, which is how a room already treats a failed read, and `"throw"` is for the callers where a short answer is worse than no answer. The snapshot passes `"throw"`; it used to raise on error and paging must not quietly take that away. Upside Arena has the same file with the same numbers, and reached it from the other direction: a scale rehearsal found four reads counting rows by fetching them, three of which became database aggregates and the rest of which page. The two apps are one design, so fix both or neither. Model output was always covered: `stripAiDashes` and `scrubAiPhrases` (`src/lib/ai/humanize-copy.ts`) run over everything Margus writes, and `AI_OPENERS` kills "it's important to note", "at the end of the day" and the "not just X, but Y" construction. Hand-written copy had no guard, which is where they had all collected. `src/lib/reader-copy.test.ts` now walks **every** `.ts`/`.tsx` under `src/` and fails on an em or en dash in any reader-facing line. It skips block comments, `//` comments (string-aware, so a URL is not mistaken for one) and `*.test.ts*`, because source is exempt: the rule is about the product. The old `SWEPT` allowlist is gone; what replaced it is `ALLOWED`, an **exception** list of five files where the character is data rather than copy (the stripper that defines it, the two prompt ban lists that name it, a `plain-error.ts` map key matched against a string the server sends, and a regex in `ForecastPanel.tsx` that parses a year range out of incoming data). Number ranges are "2 to 3 weeks" or "2028-2029", never an en dash, which is what `MARGUS_PERSONA` had been telling the model while the copy beside it did the opposite. - **Between 20:00 and 04:00 New York there is no price for a US stock, and the app says so rather than looking frozen.** Read from Tallinn that dead window is 03:00 to 11:00, which is most of a working morning, and until 2026-08-24 the portfolio simply sat there through it with no explanation, which reads as broken rather than as shut. **The window is a data fact, not a cadence bug.** Measured against Yahoo on a normal Monday: SPY and NVDA had **0** bars between 20:00 and 04:00, `ES=F` and `NQ=F` had **91** and **90**. Yahoo carries pre and post market only inside 04:00 to 20:00 (its `currentTradingPeriod` says so outright), the free chain behind it carries less, and the venues that do trade US equities overnight are licensed feeds nobody on this stack pays for. Polling harder buys the same frozen close again. **So the indication is index futures, and it never touches the value.** `OvernightNote` (`src/components/`) puts one muted line under the Overview headline: `Overnight futures: S&P 500 -0.1%, Nasdaq -0.6%. Your own prices resume at 11:00.` The portfolio number beside it stays on the last real print, because a modelled figure standing where a price goes is the one thing this app does not do (same rule that keeps `fallbackQuotes` out of `fetchQuotesWithFallback`). It is deliberately **not** beta-scaled per holding: two names with the same beta would show identical overnight moves regardless of their own news, which looks like per-name data and is not. **The server owns whether it is the window**, not the browser: `/api/market/overnight` answers `{indication: null}` outside it, and the component renders nothing for that. Deciding in the client would put the reader's device clock in charge of whether the app claims the market is shut, and it would be a second opinion competing with the session badge beside it, which reads Yahoo's own `marketState`. The resume time is minted server-side as an instant and formatted on the reader's clock, so Martin sees 11:00. **Friday 20:00 to Sunday 18:00 is deliberately not the window**: stocks are shut but so is the CME, and a two-day-old number beside the portfolio is worse than no number. The anchor is 20:00 New York on the most recent weekday, so the move answers "which way since the last price I saw"; on a Sunday that resolves to Friday's last futures bar (17:00, since the CME shut then), which is the right fallback and not a bug. A leg moving more than 25% is dropped as a contract roll or a bad bar rather than shown. `overnight.test.ts` covers every window boundary and both anchors.
- **The quote cadence is a curve, and it is two numbers, not one.** `quotePollMs` (`src/lib/market/session.ts`) is the *background* cadence: tightest at the bell and the close, quick through pre-market and after hours, 10 minutes across the overnight gap where nothing prints, 30 across the weekend, tightening again for the 45 minutes before 04:00 so the day's first pre-market print is not a whole cycle late. That slack is only affordable because of the second number. `quoteViewMaxAgeMs` is how stale a price may be **at the moment somebody is looking**, and it stays tight all night (15s open, 20s extended, 60s overnight). Every path a reader triggers, opening a room, returning to the tab, switching portfolio, the watchlist strip, is judged by `isQuoteFreshForView`; only timers ask `isQuotePollFresh`. Get this backwards and a 10 minute cycle puts a 9 minute old price in front of somebody who just opened the app, which is the failure the whole split exists to prevent. The invariant covering this used to assert five exact constants and broke on the first line of the change: it asserts the **ordering** now (extended hours stay live, the open is never slower than the sessions around it, the dead windows back off, a reader never waits on the background cadence), which is the rule, and today's numbers are free to move.

- **The phone's top bar has room for the page's one action, and everything else is a row in one menu.** Every icon button in that row is 44px wide, because `globals.css` puts a `min-width: 2.75rem` on `[data-slot="button"][data-size^="icon"]` and on `.touch-target` under `(pointer: coarse)`, which is right and is not the thing to change. - **The root element declares no overflow, or every menu hung off the chrome opens off the top of the screen.** `html` carried `overflow-x: clip` beside `body`'s, which looks like the same rule written twice and is not: setting either axis on the root makes it a scroll container, and Floating UI, which positions every Radix menu, select, popover and tooltip in this app, asks exactly that question before placing one. When the root is an overflow element it works in **document coordinates instead of viewport ones**. For anything laid out in the page the two agree, which is why nothing looked wrong for months. For the chrome they do not, because the chrome is the one thing that stays still while the page moves: the phone's top bar is `sticky top-0` and the desktop header is `md:fixed`, so each sits at the top of the screen while its place in the document stays at the top of the page, and the gap between those is the scroll offset. Measured on the running app at 390x844 scrolled 186px down, **the top bar's overflow menu opened at viewport y -132 instead of 54** -- open, focused, and entirely above the screen. What a reader does with that is tap the three dots beside their avatar, see nothing, and tap again, which closes it: a button that does nothing, twice. The phone's More menu, the desktop View and account menus and the dock's picker all had it, on both breakpoints, and only while the page was scrolled, which is what made it read as intermittent. **Sideways is still clipped, on `body` alone**: with nothing set on the root the viewport takes its overflow from `body` instead, so the page still cannot be dragged sideways (measured at 360, 390, 430, 500 and 1280: no horizontal overflow, `scrollX` stays 0). `src/lib/root-overflow.test.ts` fails if an overflow goes back on `html`, and fails the other way if `body` loses its clip.

- **The dock is chrome, not a card: `card-sheen glass glass-dock`, never `.glass` alone.** `glass-dock` (`globals.css`) replaces the body and the blur only, `--background` at **72%** and a harder blur in place of the 2% white veil and 6px, leaving the rim, the ring and the lift shadow untouched. A card is built to let the room through; a dock sits over the bottom corner, which on a phone is the brightest part of the cool lobe, and at the card's numbers it was measuring **1.88 times more coloured than the field beside it** and carrying 9.3 luminance levels across the bar. Two floors: the pane's own chroma must not exceed the field's (reached at 45%), and muted label text must clear AAA over every pixel of the bar (reached at 55%). **It is 72% rather than 55% because of a third thing, and it is the one a reader notices.** A pane that is 45% of the room under it *changes colour in one frame whenever the room does*, and nothing can ease that, because what changed is the page. Measured off a recording of the real app walking Circle to Home, the bar stepped from (17.8, 18.7, 22.2) to (10.5, 15.3, 22.0) between two frames: about a third of its own value, on a surface whose mean is in the teens. Per fill, against those two rooms: **55% steps 5.15 in luminance and 9.8 in red-minus-blue, 72% steps 3.14 and 6.0, 78% steps 2.21 and 5.0, 86% steps 1.35 and 3.0.** It keeps shrinking all the way up, so what bounds it is the other end: past roughly 78% the pane is darker than the field beside it and reads as a hole cut in the page, which is the mistake the note on `.chrome-pane` records. 72% halves the step's worst term and still carries a visible trace of the lobe. `saturate` and `brightness` were tried first and do nothing: at these luminances the whole filter chain moves the reading by at most one level, so the fill is the only lever. - **The phone bar is wordless and says the name of the room you touch, and painted labels have now been tried twice.** The rule went in after Arena hid its labels on every phone anybody owns, leaving five unlabelled glyphs; the answer is that on `pointerdown`, before the tap has finished and before the room answers, the pressed cell's name rises above the bar in the same glass and is gone inside `SAY_MS` (900ms). At rest there is not a word on screen; in use there is never a tap that does not name itself. **`pointerdown`, never `click`**: a name arriving after the tap it was meant to answer is a name nobody needed. `onFocus` fires it too, because a keyboard never presses anything. **Painted names under every glyph were built, measured and taken back out on 2026-08-30, and the arithmetic is the reason to not reach for them a third time.** The reference bar this design is judged against carries **four** destinations across ~380px, about 95px each; this one carries **six** across 374px, about **57px each**, which is 60% of the room for the same icon-over-word cell. Tuned as far as it goes (glyph 18px, gap 2px, cell padding 6px, `tracking-tight`, bar down from 60px to 52px with the cell at exactly the 44px touch floor) nothing truncated at 360, 390 or 430 and it still read as a wall of text, because six words at 12px in 374px is one however it is set. What is left past that is structural, fewer rooms or shorter words, and neither is worth what labels buy over the spoken name. **One thing was kept from that round: which room you are in is a weight.** The active glyph is `strokeWidth` 2.5 in full `--foreground` and every other cell is 1.75 in muted, on both bars. That is the reference's filled-against-outline read in the one form that survives this icon set, half of which is open paths (a line chart, a trend arrow) that fill into a blot. The accent stays spent only on news.
- **Both apps' docks are one design, and it is two shapes.** Below `md`: a hugging capsule, glyphs and no words, ending on Circle (`MobileTabBar`). At `md` and up: the same capsule, content-hugging and centred, with the label beside each glyph (`BookModeDock`). Upside Arena's `BottomDock` is the same pair and ends on the player's own face. **The material, the radius, the marker and the active weight are identical across the breakpoint; only the word count differs**, and that is the input device rather than an inconsistency: a pointer has no press to speak on, and the laptop bar carries one cell per portfolio, whose names are somebody's own words and can never be a glyph. Concentric corners: `rounded-full` shell, `p-1`, `rounded-full` cells, which is the one radius pair that stays concentric at any size.
- **The marker stretches, and that is the whole of why it reads as iOS.** A single neutral pill (`bg-foreground/10`) moves behind the cells rather than a fill appearing on one and disappearing from another, which is what makes a row of glyphs read as one place with a marker in it. What was added on 2026-08-30 is that it no longer arrives as the rectangle it left as: its **leading edge sets off before its trailing edge follows**, so it smears across the ground it covers and gathers itself back up. That means the marker is **two insets, `left` and `right`, each with its own duration** (260ms leading, 460ms trailing, `src/app/dock.css`), never a position and a width. The stretch is the transition rather than a keyframe laid over it, so it **scales with the distance**: measured on a 120px cell, one cell across peaks at 163px (1.36x), five cells across at 326px (2.72x), and both settle at exactly 120. The trailing curve overshoots by a hair, so the pill pinches to 118px on arrival before resting, which is the 2px that reads as liquid. **Edges, never a transform**: a transform cannot stretch a pill without stretching its round ends into ellipses, and both panes are absolutely positioned so nothing else is laid out. The cost that came with it is real and is guarded: a pane's width now changes on every frame of every travel, so the resize observer watches `[data-dock-cell]` rather than every child, or a measurement and a layout read run on each of those frames to answer a question about cells that have not moved. **No `backdrop-filter` on either pane**, deliberately -- iOS refracts what the pill passes over, and the web translation is a filtered element moving over content inside an already-filtered fixed bar, which is the landing page's 42-repainted-frames fault, plus a blurred label on the room you are navigating to. The marker's own two neutral hairlines (white at 10% and 4%) carry the same read for nothing. Both docks draw the panes from one component (`src/components/DockMarker.tsx`), and `dock-motion.test.ts` fails if the trailing edge stops lagging, if the leading edge ends up on the wrong side, if the panes go back to a transform, if the observer widens, or if any of it survives `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- **The two docks no longer breathe at the same moment, because they do not have the same input.** The phone bar is six glyphs, scaling it is free at every CPU throttle, and a finger has no hover to spend motion on, so **all of its motion is in the travel** and it takes the fuller breath: `DOCK_MOTION.phone` is 3% over 460ms with a 340ms travel and an 18ms lag, near the reference's own 4% over 500ms rather than half of it. Measured on the real bar: **+3.00% on both axes**, peak at 234ms (40% of its life, as traced), a -0.10% undershoot, settled by 484ms, and a one-cell move stretching the pill 1.22x over 21 painted frames with an 8.6px worst step. **The laptop bar answers a click instantly and breathes only on the pointer.** `travelMs: 0`, so `glide` writes the resting geometry and returns: the marker is simply *there* on the cell you pressed, on `pointerdown`, rather than sliding across the bar to agree with you. The travelling pill is the piece of this design that reads worst with a pointer -- a finger has nothing else going on while it waits, so a marker crossing the bar is company, but a mouse has already arrived and watching the bar take 220ms to catch up is the bar being slower than you are. The phone keeps its travel; different input, different numbers. The swell is off the click too (`swellPeak: 1`, which `swellFrames` answers with null rather than sixteen frames of `scale(1)`). Two reasons pointing the same way. A capsule that lurches every time you press it is arguing with a decision already made: you know you pressed, and the marker is already saying where you are going. And **scaling nine cells of 14px type re-rasterises them, which Chrome will not composite** -- neither `will-change` nor dropping the backdrop filter recovers it -- so the loudest motion in the bar was also the only one being paid for on every navigation. What replaced it is the gesture the Margus button makes (`hover:scale-[1.015]`): `.dock-breathe` in `dock.css`, a **transition and not an animation**, because this is a state the bar sits in for as long as it is being pointed at. It is **0.6%** and deliberately barely there -- about 3.5px an edge on a 1,164px bar, enough to say the bar noticed the pointer and no more. (It was 1.5%, measured uniform to 0.0000 points and back to exactly rest on leave, and read as too much.) **0.000px of swing across a whole press**, which is the point of it. `hoverPeak`/`hoverMs` live in `DOCK_MOTION.wide` and reach the CSS as custom properties, so every number the dock moves by is still in one file. Gated on `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` or a touch screen latches `:hover` after a tap and leaves the bar permanently 1.5% larger; there is deliberately **no `:active` rule**, since the cells have their own press and the capsule holding still under the finger is what makes that press read as landing on something solid. **Frame cost, measured over eight cycles of each:** at 1x, **zero long frames on all three** (hover swell 0/313, marker travel 0/182, phone travel 0/254, worst 16.8ms everywhere). The hover swell's own share, on against off, is 1 frame at 4x and 7 at 6x; desktops are 1x-2x machines. **Do not "simplify" this by scaling only the glass and holding the words still**: the labels still move and the bar becomes two objects rather than one. **The rim carries the glass, not the body.** `.glass-dock::after` adds three inset shadows -- a brighter near face along the top, a dark line just inside it for the thickness of the pane, and a soft bounce off the inner bottom wall. It paints once with the capsule and never animates, so it cannot drop a frame, and it is on a pseudo-element because `.card-sheen.glass` already owns that element's `box-shadow` and restating its list to append is how the two drift apart. **It is deliberately not refraction**: real liquid glass bends its backdrop, which on the web needs `backdrop-filter: url(#…)` with a displacement map -- verified to work in Chromium and not supported by WebKit, so on the iPhone this app is judged by it would do nothing, and it is a filter recomputed every frame on a fixed element, which is the landing page's own repaint fault.
- **The marker runs on the compositor, and that is what a route change was breaking.** It used to be a CSS transition on `left` and `right`, which reads well and cannot survive a navigation: those are layout properties, so every frame is laid out and painted on the main thread, and the main thread is exactly what a route change is busy with. **Measured off a recording of the real app on a phone**, tracking the pill's centre frame by frame, every travel ran two or three frames, stalled four to six while the new room rendered, then teleported the rest in one frame (+77px, +106px, +75px). Reproduced in a harness with a 180ms main-thread block at 4x CPU and read off **painted** frames (a main-thread probe cannot see this, by construction, so it is a CDP screencast with the marker recoloured to make it findable): the old build stalled mid-travel for 1, 5, 1 and 2 frames and then jumped **166px in a single frame**; the new one steps 3, 7, 10, 16, 27, 39, 34, 22, 14, 10, 7, 6, 4, 2 with one 1-frame stall, which is an ease curve rather than a freeze. `transform` is the only way out, and what it cannot do is ease two edges independently the way two transitions could, so `travelKeyframes` samples the two eased edges at **8ms** and hands them over as keyframes -- that sampling is what "more frames" means, since the browser then has a value for every frame it could draw. **The pill's width is set to its destination before the animation starts**, so `scaleX` is exactly 1 at rest and the round caps are true circles whenever it is still; they go slightly oval only in flight, which is why `MARKER_LAG_MS` is small (12ms, a one-cell move peaking at 1.16x rather than the reference's 1.29x) -- a big lag on a 48px circle reads as an egg. Never put dock geometry back on `left`/`right`, and never let the resting `scaleX` be anything but 1.
- **The capsule breathes, and the WHOLE bar breathes as one object. This is the phone bar's motion now, and only the phone bar's** (the laptop breathes on the pointer instead; see the bullet above). It is a uniform scale, and a one-axis scale is the thing to never do.** Traced frame by frame off the reference recording at 30fps, fitting the capsule's own four edges sub-pixel: width and height move together and by the same fraction (n53 +1.96%/+2.04%, n56 **+3.99%/+3.95%**, n58 +2.66%/+2.31%, n60 +0.67%/+0.68%), symmetric about the centre on both axes -- at the peak the left edge moved -23.8px against the right's +23.7px and the top -3.8px against the bottom +3.9px. So `transform: scale()` on the capsule itself, contents included, no `transform-origin`, no lean. **Two earlier readings of this were wrong and both are worth knowing.** A vertical measuring window that missed the capsule's real edges reported the height as a constant 234px when the true height is 187px, and that one number produced a `scaleX`; scaling on one axis stretches letterforms sideways, which is what makes the bar feel wrong, where a uniform scale magnifies type instead of distorting it. Then, over-correcting, the glass was moved to its own layer so only the material scaled -- also wrong, because the reference plainly moves everything together and a bar whose rim slides while its words stand still is two objects, not one. **The shape is a swell, not a snap**: normalised against its own peak it runs 0, 0.26, 0.35, 0.49, 0.74, 0.94, **1.00 at 40% of its life**, 0.90, 0.67, 0.41, 0.17, 0.05, then **under rest to -0.035** before settling, over 500ms. The undershoot is the point; an ease with the peak at 11% and no undershoot is a flinch. Samples are one recorded frame apart so they are interpolated linearly, because the curve is carried by the data and an easing on top would be a guess about a measured shape. **The marker's two edges take the same duration and the same curve, and the trailing one is simply delayed** (350ms, `cubic-bezier(0.5, 0.2, 0.05, 0.95)` fitted numerically to the reference's own pill to sse 0.0011, 28ms lag): a constant lag is the back of a blob following the front at a fixed distance, which is why the reference reads as one object, where two different durations read as a rectangle being stretched. Measured against the recording, a one-cell move peaks at **1.28x at t=133ms** against the reference's **1.29x at t=133ms**. A six-cell walk reaches 2.5x, which is the same physics at six times the velocity and is left alone. **The cost is real and lands in one place**: scaling text re-rasterises it, so the labelled laptop bar is free at 1x and 4x CPU throttling (0 and 1 long frames) and costs at 6x (30 against 4) and 10x (78 of 150 against 19); `will-change: transform` does not recover it. The glyph-only phone bar is free at every throttle. Desktops are 1x-2x machines, so this was the right trade while the laptop bar paid it at all; it does not any more, and the shape below now runs on the glyph-only bar it was traced from.
- **The marker leaves on the press, and the route only confirms it.** `activeId` is read from `usePathname()` (`Dashboard`), so every bit of the dock's motion used to be downstream of the App Router committing: the bar was tied to the network rather than to the finger, and prefetching only makes that wait short rather than making it not a wait. iOS moves its indicator on touch-down, and `useDockMarker` does now: a `pointerdown` on a cell sets `aimed`, `measure` positions the marker at `aimed.current ?? on`, and the router arriving on that same cell simply clears the bet with the marker already there. **It is a bet, so it has to be able to lose, and it loses three ways**: the release landed somewhere other than the cell it started on (a press dragged off is not a tap), the room answered with a different cell or the cell stopped existing, or nothing answered at all within `AIM_GIVES_UP_MS`. That last number is **4000ms and is deliberately long** -- snapping the marker home mid-wait looks far more broken than letting it stand where the reader put it. Only a plain primary press bets: a middle click or a held modifier opens the room in **another tab**, and a marker moving for a room this tab is not in is the one way this is worse than waiting. Only `[data-dock-goes]` cells bet, so the add cell (a dialog) and the folded picker (a menu) never move it. Meter tracks, heatmap and data cells, skeletons, step dots and `focus:bg-muted` stay opaque on purpose. Overlays — menus, selects, popovers, dialogs, sheets, drawers — are `.glass-overlay`, the same material at a much heavier fill (`--popover` at 88% against a card's 45%), because they sit *over* content and an element with `backdrop-filter` does not blur what another one painted, so a menu under the chrome sees the market strip unblurred. Glass carries three specular terms (top hairline, a soft inset falloff under it, bottom hairline); the falloff is the refraction and on a card it is a `box-shadow`, never a `background-image`, because `.veil-hover` would wipe a gradient on hover. `.chrome-pane` is the one exception and uses a gradient on purpose — it is a band of two different heights and a shadow spread is a fixed distance, so it would hug the edge on one and wash half the band on the other; nothing in the chrome hovers.
- **The phone is its own room, not a narrow desktop — full account in `DESIGN_TOKENS.md`.** Below `md` the ambient lobes come in and up, and glass thins, so the corner light reaches through the cards; the phone chrome is **one element**, not one look — `AppHeader`'s `.chrome-pane` holds the phone top bar, the desktop header row and the market strip, because a `backdrop-filter` samples its own slice of the backdrop and two stacked panes land at different tones with a seam between them (`MobileTopBar` is a bare row now; giving it a fill or blur again brings the seam back). The only edge is at the pane's bottom, as on desktop; the page gutter, `PANEL_PAD`, `NESTED_PAD` and `SCORE_CELL` step to 16px below `sm` and figures to `text-xl`. Hard rules: **figures wrap, never `whitespace-nowrap`** (that is how `23.0% a year` escaped its card); **a small card fitting two pairs of values uses two spread rows, never two stacked columns**, sized to fit with `flex-wrap` as the floor (columns share one width, so the `min-w-0` side starves and clips); **a ticker chip heading a column of text gets `.chip-hang`**, opt-in and never on `Badge` itself; put the unit in the sub-line and pass `Scoreboard mobileCols={1}` for any row whose cells carry a sentence. `overscroll-behavior-y: none` on `html, body` is load-bearing — without it WebKit's bounce drags the sticky top bar down and slices the tab bar in half. **When checking for clipping, measure against the nearest clipping ancestor's padding box**, not just `scrollWidth` and the viewport; `.chip-hang` is the one sanctioned exception. Touch targets are pointer-gated in `globals.css` and cover buttons, inputs, selects and `[data-slot="select-trigger"]` — but **never `.inline-edit`** (table rows are a fixed `h-10`) and never the checkbox/switch pseudo (it would steal taps from the rows either side). A size class on an `<h1>`–`<h4>` works now that the element rules are in `@layer base`; `src/lib/heading-scale.test.ts` fails if one disagrees with the scale.
- **Pull the page down for new numbers, and it is drawn by hand because the browser's own was switched off.** `overscroll-behavior-y: none` is the same switch for the WebKit bounce and for the browser's pull to refresh, so killing the bounce (which dragged the sticky top bar down and sliced the tab bar in half) killed the gesture with it, and there is no setting that keeps one and not the other. That cost more than it looked: the app runs standalone, so there is no address bar and therefore **no reload button anywhere in it**, and the status strip reports "Prices, 12s ago" to somebody who could do nothing whatever about the answer. Prices do poll themselves here, on the curve `quotePollMs` draws and refetched whenever a reader arrives at a screen, so this is almost never about numbers the reader does not already have; what they did not have was any way to **ask**, and a reading you can ask to be re-taken is a reading you can trust. `PullToRefresh` (`src/components/`, mounted in `Providers` so its ring sits outside every room's transform) is the bounce put back under our own control, moving the room's own `<main>` and leaving the chrome exactly where it is. **It needs that CSS line rather than fighting it**: with the bounce gone a downward drag at the top scrolls nothing, so there is nothing to prevent, every touch listener is passive, `touchmove` is attached only for the length of a touch that could become a pull, and nothing is attached at all unless `(any-pointer: coarse)` matches. One `requestAnimationFrame` write per frame, all of it transform and opacity plus one dash offset on a 22px circle, and **zero React renders between the finger going down and the work starting**. **The page is found from the touch, never from the route** (`closest("main")`), because `WorkspaceShell` keeps every visited room mounted and hidden, so there is more than one `<main>` in this document at almost any moment. **The pull reaches only the room on screen**: `requestWorkspaceRefresh` / `onWorkspaceRefresh` (`workspace-rooms.ts`) gate on `isWorkspaceRoomActive` exactly as every poller in those rooms already does, since shouting at five mounted rooms would put five rooms' worth of fetches on the free-tier providers in one gesture; a room with nothing to refetch (Account, Admin) answers `handled: false` and the router is asked for the page instead, because a ring that draws and quietly does nothing is worse than no gesture at all, the reader now believing they have checked. The feel lives in `src/lib/pull-to-refresh.ts` with a test around every number: travel is `CEILING * (1 - e^(-raw / CEILING))`, whose derivative at zero is exactly **1**, so the first pixel of the pull is the first pixel of the finger and the page feels attached rather than lagging; 84px of finger buys the 56px that arms it, and the ceiling is 96 so there is still give left when the ring completes, which is what tells a thumb it has arrived rather than hit the end of a track. The ring fades in over the first 40% and reaches full size exactly as it completes, so there is no frame where anything appears or jumps; the only animated paint in the whole gesture is the arc unwinding into a turning quarter and closing again when the answer lands. **Under reduced motion the ring does not turn**, it is drawn whole and held still, and the pull itself is untouched, because direct manipulation is not the motion that setting is asking about. The ring is `position: fixed` and that is safe **only** while it carries no `backdrop-filter`: fixed plus a backdrop filter over moving content is the landing page fault measured at 42 repainted frames, and a pull is by definition content moving under something pinned to the window. Upside Arena has the same component, the same curve and the same numbers, and got there from the other side, since nothing in that app refetches at all. Fix both or neither.
- **A forecast on a phone is a shape first, and it never drops a year.** The holding cards were a two-column grid of five editable prices with today's price sitting in it as a sixth peer, and they filtered the last forecast year out so the columns divided evenly (`mobileYears = yearCols.filter((y) => y !== 2030)`) while the percentage in each card's own corner was measured **to that dropped year** — so `$CRWV 382.1%` sat above a card whose last figure was `+290%`, and a card that cannot add up to its own headline is worse than one that says less. The whole-portfolio block had the same hole: its `To End 2030` line was `hidden md:block` inside a `md:hidden` container, which is to say dead since it was written. Time also ran in a Z, 2026 top right and 2027 bottom left, because two columns give a sequence two directions. Now each card leads with `TickerSpark`, a 56px path from today to the last year with a dashed rule at today's price so above and below read without a number being involved, and one spread row naming both ends; **Show every year** opens `YearRail`, which lists Now and every year down the page with that year's gain beside it. The card went from about 700px to about 230px, so four holdings fit on a phone where one used to, which is the whole point of a five-year forecast: comparing names. **Deliberately not `SheetPathChart`** — that one belongs to the portfolio and carries an axis, a drag readout and 224px of height, and five stacked would be taller than the grid they replaced. The rail's price fields are `EoyPriceInput fill`, **bounded at `w-24`, never `w-full`**: the edit cue is a dashed `border-bottom` on the element itself, so a full-width field draws a rule clean across the row and reads as a separator, which `globals.css` warns about in as many words. Height sits on the row (`min-h-12`), not the input, because an input given a 44px min-height centres its digits and leaves the dash floating ten pixels below them. `src/lib/forecast-years.test.ts` fails on a hardcoded forecast year or on a second, narrower list of years anywhere in the panel: the years are a range that moves, and a layout that only fits four of them is a layout problem, never a reason to show three quarters of a forecast.
